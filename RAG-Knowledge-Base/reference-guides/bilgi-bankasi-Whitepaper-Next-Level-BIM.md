# "NEXT LEVEL" BIM: Achieving an Integrated Working Method in Seven Steps
**Whitepaper by ALLPLAN**

## Introduction
Digital design with BIM is continuing to advance everywhere. In addition to the benefits that architects, engineers, and specialist designers can see – such as a high level of efficiency and maximum cost reliability in the design and building phase – it is the clients and real estate operators who are recognizing the added value of building information modeling with increasing awareness.

However, there are only a few regulations at the international level that shape the digital design method in projects. Construction is anchored in the respective country’s DNA, creating diverse professional profiles for architects, engineers, and specialist designers. BIM must account for these social characteristics while defining design processes that have international validity.

## 1. Developing Universal Design Rules
Internationally valid and binding regulations are desperately needed for the BIM planning process. One important basic parameter that buildingSMART, standards committees, and software manufacturers are increasingly adopting is the so-called **BIM Level**. It defines the depth of BIM implementation within a project. Different levels are defined for this purpose – currently from 0 to iBIM in Level 3, with Level 4 and beyond expected in the future.

## 2. Defining the BIM Design Depth in Development Stages
In BIM levels 0 to 3, the development stages of construction design can be placed in an evolutionary order:

- **Level 0 (CAD):** Purely 2D-oriented CAD based solely on graphic elements (Drawings/Paper).
- **Level 1 (2D/3D Hybrid):** Combines 2D and 3D information, facilitating an understanding of the draft and specialized designs. Simple collision checks are possible locally using Files.
- **Level 2 (BIM - Building Information Management):** A consistent, object-based working method with specialist structured models enriched with information. It enables quantities/volumes derivation, logistics schedules, and cooperation among designers. It creates transparency and cost reliability through a coordination model. This level requires openBIM for independent data exchange.
- **Level 3 (BLM - Building Lifecycle Management):** An integral working method based on a database. Transactable, interoperable data through an integrated web services data hub.

## 3. Supporting the Database-Oriented Design Process
BIM Level 3 is characterized by an integral working method based on a single database. Design intentions are communicated transparently in a model-based fashion via the manufacturer-neutral **BIM Collaboration Format (BCF)**. This makes it easier to rectify design errors, document building defects, and dramatically increase project quality.

Level 3 takes the building operation into consideration in addition to planning and the building phase. The building lifecycle has a complete continuity of data flow for the first time in BIM Level 3.

## 4. Considering Diversity with National Adjustments
Various industrial nations have been working intensively with the BIM method and the definition of the BIM levels. These include the United Kingdom, Scandinavian countries, the USA, and Japan. International tenders (e.g., above EU thresholds) necessitate these standardized approaches to ensure fair and feasible collaboration across borders.
